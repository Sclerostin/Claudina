Edge Reader

One method for every chart. On each 5-minute candle it checks 97 conditions from the trading literature and adds up what each one has been worth in past data. The result is the expected value of a trade right now, in R, after costs. It says BUY when that number clears your bar, SELL when the trade ends, and WAIT the rest of the time.

expected R = day part + 5-minute part − costs

Illustrative reading. The labels and layout are what the script draws; the numbers change on every candle.

BUY

Expected R for a long reaches your minimum (+0.05R by default). It enters at the candle's close with a stop 1.5 ATR below and a target at 2R, and the label shows the expected R.

SELL

The trade ends: at the target, at the stop, or at 3:55 PM. The label shows the R it made. With shorts switched on, SHORT and COVER work the same way.

The method

Every condition is a yes/no fact you can see on a chart. Each has points: how much better or worse trades went, in R, when it was true. The points add up to the expected R.

Trend

Daily trend and the 200-day average, intraday trend, SPY's trend.

Dow, Elder's triple screen

Location

Distance from VWAP in standard deviations, the day's premium or discount, opening range, yesterday's levels, stretch from the average.

Dalton, Shannon, ICT

Phase

Tight range (accumulation), a low swept and reclaimed (spring), a high swept and rejected (upthrust), a break of structure.

Wyckoff, ICT AMD

Volume

Volume against the same time of day, climax candles, whether buyers or sellers carried the last candles.

Wyckoff effort vs result, Aziz

Momentum and strength

RSI(2) and RSI(14), 3-day runs, strength against SPY today and over 20 days.

Connors, Bellafiore

Market and calendar

SPY's gap, oversold or overbought state, time of day, Mondays, Fridays, the turn of the month.

Crabel, calendar studies

The method is judged only on data it hasn't seen: points are fitted on the past and scored on the next year. A table that can't predict the next year doesn't get used.

What the evidence says

Days it rated better really were better

Daily points, fitted on earlier years and scored on each next year, 2017 to 2026. Stock-days sorted into tenths by expected value; columns show what longs actually made from the open to the close, in R (half a daily ATR), after slippage.

So, for now, mostly WAIT

The daily reading ranks days correctly in every unseen year, but the edge it finds is smaller than the cost of a 2R day trade. On the 5-minute chart, weights learned from one month predicted the opposite the next, and textbook weights worked in trending months and reversed in choppy ones. Seven months of 5-minute data can't settle that. Your TradingView history can.

Teach it with your data

Measure mode turns Deep Backtesting into a data collector. It takes up to two trades a day at set times, whatever the conditions, and writes every condition into the trade's signal name. Those trades show what each condition is worth over years.

  1. Open a stock's 5-minute chart with Edge Reader. In its settings set Mode to Measure long.

  2. Open the Strategy Tester, turn on Deep Backtesting, set the start as far back as it goes, and generate the report.

  3. In List of trades, export the data as CSV. The export is on the TradingView website or desktop app. Name the file after the stock, for example NVDA.csv.

  4. Repeat for 10 to 20 stocks. Do Measure short too if you might ever short.

  5. Send me the files, or run python refit.py *.csv --write in research/. New 5-minute points are written into both scripts only if they predict the later years they weren't fitted on.

  6. Copy the updated scripts into TradingView and set Mode back to Trade.

As a check, I ran the same loop on 7 months of 2026 data from 47 stocks. It refused the new points: they did worse than average on the unseen months. That's the safeguard working.

Install

  1. Edge Reader

    Copy it below. In TradingView open the Pine Editor, create a new strategy, delete everything, paste and save. Line 1 must read //@version=6. Add it to a 5-minute chart, regular hours only.

  2. Your risk

    In its settings, under Risk, enter your account size and risk per trade. Under Properties, set Initial capital to the same amount. Under Costs, set the slippage you expect per fill.

  3. Edge Radar

    Create a new indicator the same way and paste Edge Radar. Put it on SPY's 5-minute chart and list your stocks. It ranks them by today's expected R from the daily part, after each stock's costs.

Alerts run on TradingView's servers, so they fire with the app closed. Set them to expire as late as your plan allows.

Execute

By hand

  1. Tap the BUY notification. It names the shares, stop and target.

  2. Long-press the chart and tap + to create an order with your connected moomoo account.

  3. Buy, Limit, at the alert price, with Take profit and Stop loss turned on.

  4. At 3:55 PM, close anything still open.

With the bridge

A small program on a computer that stays on places the Orders alerts in moomoo through OpenAPI.

  • Refuses anything below your minimum expected R.
  • Limit entries that never chase more than 0.15%.
  • A stop at moomoo the moment shares fill.
  • Closes everything at 3:55 PM by its own clock.
  • 3 open positions at most; stops for the day at −3R; shorts off unless you allow them.

Dry run first, then paper, then live and small: moomoo_bridge/README.md.
